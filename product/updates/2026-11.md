## The inspection bay

Any domain can be graded before you send a single message, with no signup.
relay checks the records that decide whether mail lands, from SPF and DMARC to
MTA-STS and the blocklists, and grades the setup from S to F. Each failure comes
with its fix. A blocking breakage pins the domain to F. The result joins a
public list ordered by tier and carries a share card you can post.

## The customs post

Mail crosses borders, and every hop answers to a flag. Control of the route is
part of digital sovereignty. A provider in another country follows that
country's law, and can be compelled to hand over the mail it holds. So the check
names the countries that control a domain's mail: the mailbox and sending
providers behind the MX and SPF records, and the networks behind them. A mailbox
in Frankfurt and a sender in Virginia read as two flags, and the detail lists
every provider and network behind each one. A hostile power on the route shows
up before you send a single message.

## The standing watch

Your custom domains stand a watch. relay reruns the checks every hour and
compares the result with the last run. When a record breaks or recovers, your
organization gets an email with the new tier, the checks that now fail, and the
checks that pass again, each with its fix. The public checker can start the same
watch for a domain it already graded.

## The briefing

AI-assisted coding works best when the agent knows the connection. One click on
the credentials or inbound email page copies a Markdown brief with the
connection values, the environment variables, and a complete Django and Next.js
example. The receiving brief also covers the webhook signature and the
attachment download. Paste it into your agent's prompt and it wires the app
without a round of questions.

## Supplemental log

Improvements and smaller items from the same month, logged for the record.
Temporary refusals now show as soft bounces, apart from hard bounces, so the
message log and the reputation chart name the cause. Sandbox messages carry a
blue flask badge from the start. The reports list adds a stacked volume chart
and a domain filter, and report details name relay's own source addresses with a
hostname and network. The domain setup page splits the records into sending,
receiving, and reporting tabs, each with a zone file. MCP clients read every
transmission leg of a message. Webhook deliveries now use the Standard Webhooks
signature, so the official SDKs verify them. Copy the new `whsec_` secret from
each endpoint and switch your verification code. Links to titled pages get a
generated share card, and the error pages now carry their own copy and
illustrations. A login attempt without credentials now gets a normal
authentication error.

open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/codingjoe/relay/issues). For the
full reference, read the <a href="{% url 'docs:list' %}">docs</a>.
